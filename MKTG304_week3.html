Welcome students and frame the session as an applied client workflow. The point today is not to generate polished marketing outputs as quickly as possible. The point is to learn a careful AI-assisted process: gather evidence, prompt well, review critically, revise, and then use the result to make better content decisions for Music Planet. Emphasise that the work will be done in a single chatbot conversation where each output becomes context for the next step.

Content That Earns Attention

Music Planet wants to sell more instruments, but it does not want every post to feel like an advertisement.
What could Music Planet publish that would be genuinely useful to musicians while still supporting future sales?
Think: advice
Think: confidence
Think: trust
Expected answers might include beginner buying guides, staff gear explainers, setup checklists, local musician stories, home studio tips, repair or troubleshooting content, and comparison guides. Push students to distinguish between content that simply announces a deal and content that helps a customer make a better decision. A useful follow-up is: How might this content support a sale without sounding like a sales pitch?

Creating Content for Search

A parent who knows very little about instruments searches:
“What guitar should I buy for a beginner?”
What should Music Planet’s answer include to be more useful than a typical product page?
Listen for answers about size, age or height, playability, budget, durability, tuning stability, accessories, lessons, when to choose acoustic versus electric, and why in-store advice or setup matters. The teaching point is that search-led content should answer the real question behind the query, not just push a product listing. This also primes students for thinking about buyer journeys later.

Deciding Whether Content Worked

Music Planet publishes a home-studio guide. It receives strong views and engagement, but relatively few immediate sales.
What evidence would you examine before deciding whether the content was successful?
AttentionEngagementTrustAction
This question introduces measurement without becoming technical. Students may mention views, watch time, saves, comments, shares, website visits, product page views, enquiries, store visits, assisted conversions, email signups, or repeat visits. The point is that content can have value before the final transaction, but that does not mean all engagement is meaningful. Ask: What would make this evidence stronger?

Section 1

Building a Buyer Persona

Before we ask AI to build personas, we need to decide how many personas Music Planet may actually need.

Audience structure → template → persona → validation
Transition from the content audit. Explain that students may have noticed several possible audiences: experienced musicians, beginners, parents, home producers, deal seekers, or customers with urgent gear problems. But those observations are not yet personas. First, we need to determine which groups are meaningfully different from a marketing perspective.

Why Audience Understanding Comes First

A weak persona
is a decorative profile with invented details.
A useful persona
changes what you say, where you say it, and what evidence you provide.

Decision rule

Create a separate persona only when the group is different enough that Music Planet would need to change its messaging, content, channel approach or purchase support.

Different questions?
Different confidence barriers?
Different purchase triggers?
Different content needs?
This slide prevents students from assuming that every audience label deserves a separate persona. A persona is not a taxonomy. It is a decision-making tool. If two customer groups need essentially the same message, same reassurance, same content and same purchase support, then separating them may add complexity without strategic value.

What Evidence Do We Already Have?

Client voice

Digital Marketing Brief

Goals, audience descriptions, positioning, brand voice, current activity and constraints.

Research lens

Client Intelligence Brief

Market context, competitor evidence, customer behaviour and external opportunity signals.

Customer view

Website and social presence

What customers actually encounter: product categories, messaging, advice, imagery and calls to action.

Key idea: These sources do not automatically tell us the personas. They give the chatbot evidence to reason from.
Emphasise the different roles of the three sources. The marketing brief tells us how the client sees itself. The Client Intelligence Brief adds external context. The website and social channels show what the market currently sees. A good prompt should help AI compare these sources, not just summarise one of them.

Exercise

Continue the Same Chat: Build One Persona

12 to 15 minutesOne persona at a time

Iteration task

Stay in the same chatbot conversation. Use the audience groups already identified, paste or summarise your selected template, then ask the chatbot to build only the first persona.

Context: existing evidence + selected group + template Role: persona and customer-research strategist Action: develop one persona Format: follow the chosen template Target audience: Music Planet + campaign team
Ask the chatbot to pause after the first persona so you can review and refine it before generating the next one.
The key here is controlled iteration. If students ask for all personas at once, they will likely receive shallow, overconfident profiles. One-at-a-time generation makes it easier to review whether the persona is grounded in the evidence. Remind students to include the instruction that the chatbot should avoid inventing precise demographics or behaviours unless those are supported by the supplied context.

10 minutesCritical thinking iteration

Unsupported assumption
Questionable or contradicted
Persona claimEvidence sourceConfidenceRevision
Claim from the personaBrief, research, website, inference or noneHigh / medium / lowKeep, qualify, investigate or remove
Critical thinking means challenging the chatbot’s confidence, not asking it to add more detail.
This is one of the most important slides in the session. Tell students that they cannot fully prove persona accuracy without primary research. What they can do is assess whether each claim is supported, reasonably inferred, unsupported, or questionable. Encourage follow-up prompts such as: What evidence supports this claim? Which source did it come from? Are you confusing possibility with fact? What should be revised or softened?
Section 2

Building the Buyer Journey

A persona tells us who the customer is. A journey shows how they move toward a decision.

One persona · one journey · one content opportunity map
Transition from persona validation to application. Make clear that students will not build a journey for every persona today. The priority is to learn the method by building one journey carefully. If the persona is weak or unsupported, the journey will be weak too.
MKTG 304 · Week 3
Buyer journey

Why the Buyer Journey Matters

TriggerA need or problem appears
ResearchThe customer looks for answers
EvaluationOptions are compared
PurchaseA decision is made
Post-purchaseConfidence or regret forms

No universal journey

A parent buying a child’s first guitar, a producer building a home studio, and a gigging musician replacing failed gear may all interact with Music Planet differently.

This is a quick recap, not a long lecture. The distinction is simple: personas are about the customer; journeys are about the customer’s movement through a decision. Different personas can have different triggers, information needs, anxieties, channels and moments of friction. This directly links back to content marketing because content should answer the questions that appear at each stage.

Final Synthesis

Before you leave, complete these three statements:

Our selected Music Planet persona is...
The most important moment in their journey is...
The content opportunity Music Planet should prioritise is...
Next step: turn the journey insights into specific content ideas and campaign recommendations.
Use this as the final debrief if time allows. It forces students to compress the work into a practical strategic takeaway. If time runs short, make this an exit task or a quick pair-share. The point is to close the loop: content audit led to audience thinking; audience thinking led to personas; persona thinking led to journey mapping; journey mapping revealed content opportunities.
