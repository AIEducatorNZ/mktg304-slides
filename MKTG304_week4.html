Opening Discussion

Engagement Questions

Three general SEO questions, applied to Music Planet, before we open any tools. We have not audited the site yet, so these are about how you think, not what the data says.
On-page  ·  Off-page  ·  Technical   |   2 to 3 minutes each
These warm up SEO thinking across the three pillars we investigate today: on-page, off-page, and technical. Keep them open and discussion-led. There is no audit data yet, so the point is reasoning, not recall. Each question is a chip opportunity, aim to get every attending student contributing at least once across the three.
Discussion 1 of 3
On-Page
Someone searches "beginner guitar NZ" and lands on a Music Planet product page. Beyond price and specs, what would that page itself need to actually answer the question and earn the ranking?
Discussion 2 of 3
Off-Page
Music Planet and a competitor list the same guitar at the same price, but one consistently outranks the other in Google. If the pages are equally good, what off-page factors might explain the gap?
Discussion 3 of 3
Technical
Music Planet's online store keeps adding product pages, many of them nearly identical. What technical problems can quietly build up and drag on search performance as the catalogue grows?

Step 1 of 9 · Set Up & Baseline

Create your Music Planet project

You already have a SEMrush account. The first step is to create a dedicated Project for Music Planet, so that every analysis tool you configure, Site Audit, Position Tracking and the rest, lives in one place.

Click Projects, then Create new project. Enter musicplanet.co.nz as the domain, using the bare domain without the www prefix, and name the project "Music Planet NZ". This container will hold everything you set up across the semester.

Why this matters: a project keeps your Music Planet analysis organised and separate from any other domain you explore.
Common issues at this step. Students entering www.musicplanet.co.nz, advise the bare domain, SEMrush detects the canonical version. Students who cannot find the Projects tab, it is in the left sidebar and sometimes collapsed, look for the folder icon. Limited plans may cap the number of projects, if students hit a limit they should delete any test projects created during onboarding. Confirm everyone can see the Music Planet project on their dashboard before Step 2. The project name is a convention, students can use any name, but consistency helps when you refer to it in later tutorials.
Step 2 of 9 · Set Up & Baseline

Capture the baseline SEO footprint

Before configuring any tools, open Domain Overview and enter musicplanet.co.nz. Set the country database to New Zealand. Review the organic traffic estimate, the number of ranking keywords, the top countries driving traffic, and the main competitors SEMrush lists.

Click into Top Organic Keywords, then View details, to see the full keyword list and current rankings. Take a screenshot. This is your baseline before any deeper configuration.

Why this matters: the Domain Overview shows where Music Planet stands in search today, your reference point for everything that follows.
Walk through the metrics. Organic Traffic is a model, not GA data, SEMrush estimates it from click-through rates applied to keyword rankings. Authority Score (0 to 100) is a composite of backlink profile, organic traffic and spam signals, similar to Moz's Domain Authority. Ask students, before they look at the data, what they expect Music Planet to rank for, this primes strategic thinking rather than passive recording. The New Zealand database matters, Music Planet is an NZ business, so national data is more relevant than global. Screenshot prompt: save the Domain Overview now, it is your Assignment 2 baseline data point.
Step 3 of 9 · Core Diagnostics

Run your first Site Audit

In your project dashboard, click Site Audit and create a campaign for musicplanet.co.nz. In Settings, set the crawl limit to 5,000 pages, choose Website as the source, and set the frequency to weekly.

Launch it and keep moving, it runs in the background and takes roughly 5 to 30 minutes. When it finishes, review the overall health score, errors, warnings, and thematic reports to identify technical and on-page issues.

Retail note: a Shopify store carries many product and collection URLs, so crawl scope matters more here than it would for a simple brochure site. The Site Audit is the foundation of any SEO analysis.
Launch the audit at the start of this step and move forward, students return to review results during Steps 5 to 7. Crawl limit of 5,000 is ample. Crawl source Website is correct, Sitemap is an alternative if you know the sitemap URL. Weekly frequency suits a monitoring setup. When it completes, direct students to the three headline metrics: Health Score, Errors in red, Warnings in yellow. Issues to anticipate on a music retail store, framed as things to look for rather than confirmed facts: missing image alt text, image-heavy pages loading slowly, thin or duplicate content across similar product pages, and duplicate meta descriptions. Remind students an audit is a diagnostic, not a verdict, every site has issues, what matters is whether they are significant and whether Music Planet is addressing them.
Step 4 of 9 · Core Diagnostics

Track where Music Planet ranks

In the project dashboard, click Position Tracking. Enter musicplanet.co.nz, then configure: search engine Google, device desktop and mobile, location New Zealand. Add an initial keyword set by importing from the Domain Overview results you reviewed in Step 2.

Add Music Planet's two main NZ competitors to benchmark against. Save and start tracking. Rankings will populate within 24 hours.

therockshop.co.nz musicworks.co.nz
Why this matters: Position Tracking turns a one-off keyword snapshot into an ongoing view, so you can measure the impact of any strategy you recommend.
Competitor domains: therockshop.co.nz and musicworks.co.nz, the two main NZ specialist music retailers. Keyword import from Domain Overview, students should import the top 20 to 30 keywords by traffic and refine after the keyword step. Mobile versus desktop, Google uses mobile-first indexing so mobile is the primary signal, but desktop often differs, running both gives the full picture. Rankings take 24 hours, a good moment to stress that SEO is not instant. Ask students why tracking competitors alongside Music Planet is useful, expected answer: relative movement, if Music Planet improves but competitors improve faster, the net position may not have changed.
Step 5 of 9 · Core Diagnostics

Build Music Planet's keyword universe

Phase 1 · AnswerThePublic
Paste musicplanet.co.nz into AnswerThePublic and review the Suggested For You keywords and content ideas it generates from the site. Optionally, run one seed such as "guitar" or "digital piano" for the deeper Search Cloud of real questions. Shortlist the most relevant, realistic terms.
Phase 2 · SEMrush
Take your shortlist into Keyword Overview and the Keyword Magic Tool, set the NZ database, and check volume, difficulty and CPC to decide what is worth targeting. Add one head term manually, "guitars nz" or "musical instruments nz", for contrast.
One tool surfaces the questions people ask; the other tells you which are worth answering. Different tools, different jobs.
The point of this step is tool familiarity and a clean handoff. AnswerThePublic's free tier allows three searches a day and unlocks fully once students register with a simple Google login, so they should choose their seeds deliberately rather than burning searches by trial and error. It shows no volume, difficulty or CPC, which is exactly why the exercise then moves to SEMrush. Check the URL "Suggested For You" feature works on a plain free account before class, the fallback is a manual seed such as "guitar". Worked example from Music Planet's actual URL output. Commercial or product-intent terms to test for winnability: "digital piano under 1000 nz", "beginner synth nz", "electronic drum kit nz", "guitar pedals nz", and the very specific "nux mg30 nz", an ultra-long-tail product term that is almost certainly low difficulty and high intent, showing why the very long tail is often where a smaller retailer actually wins. Content and informational ideas that double as blog or guide topics and tie back to the Week 3 journey work: "acoustic vs electric guitar nz", "home studio setup on a budget", "best beginner keyboards nz". Branded: "music planet nz review", the case where you would expect Music Planet to rank already. Then the manually added head term, "musical instruments nz" or "guitars nz", which will look tempting in the suggestions and fall apart on difficulty, that is the teaching contrast: a smaller retailer wins on the specific, winnable long tail, not the head term. On difficulty bands, realistic targets sit lower, very high KD terms are dominated by big-authority sites. NZ volumes look small because the market is small, 50 to 200 a month can still be meaningful.
Step 6 of 9 · Core Diagnostics

Identify page-level improvements

In your project, open On Page SEO Checker. Add Music Planet's key pages, starting with the homepage, the top category or collection pages, and a few key product pages, and map your target keywords to each.

Run the tool for suggestions on content improvements, semantic keyword usage, and on-page optimisation. Cross-reference these against the Site Audit issues for the same URLs, a page flagged for thin content in the audit may also be under-optimised here.

Why this matters: this is where diagnostics become specific, actionable recommendations, exactly what your audit needs to demonstrate.
Key pages to add: the homepage, a top category page such as guitars or keyboards, the delivery or pricing page if relevant, and a couple of high-value product pages. Map keywords from Step 5 to the most relevant page, for example a beginner-guitar term to the relevant category or product page. The Checker generates three types of recommendation, Strategy, Ideas and Technical, focus on the Ideas tab first, that is where the actionable content suggestions live. Cross-referencing with the Site Audit: if the audit flags a page for low word count and the Checker recommends semantic keywords, these are the same problem viewed from two angles. Assignment 2 note: when writing up the audit, cite both tools where they corroborate each other, that shows triangulated analysis rather than a list of tool outputs.
Step 7 of 9 · Benchmark & Monitor

Understand Music Planet's link profile

Open Backlink Analytics and enter musicplanet.co.nz. Review total backlinks, referring domains, and authority metrics. Then open Backlink Audit within your project to identify any toxic or low-quality links.

Look at the backlink profiles of Rockshop and MusicWorks to spot outreach or digital PR opportunities, sites linking to a competitor but not to Music Planet represent a gap worth noting.

NZ music blogs & media musician communities gear-review sites school & education resources
Why this matters: off-page authority affects how well Music Planet ranks; its backlink health completes the picture.
Key metrics in Backlink Analytics: Referring Domains matters more than total backlinks, 100 links from 100 different domains beats 1,000 from one. The Authority Score of referring domains, higher is more valuable. The follow versus nofollow ratio. Toxic score, SEMrush assigns a 0 to 100 toxicity score, above roughly 45 is flagged as potentially harmful. The Backlink Gap against competitors is the most strategically useful output, a site linking to Rockshop and MusicWorks but not Music Planet is a potential digital PR target. Ask students what types of site would link to a music retailer, expected answers: NZ music and business media, musician forums and communities, gear-review blogs, and school or community-music resources.
Step 8 of 9 · Benchmark & Monitor

Compare competitors in the right tools

Position Tracking
Add Rockshop and MusicWorks as competitors, then compare visibility, ranking positions, keyword movement, and relative performance over time. This shows who is gaining or losing ground in the search results you are actively monitoring.
Backlinks & Backlink Gap
Compare referring domains, link authority, and backlink profiles. Use Backlink Gap to find sites that link to Rockshop or MusicWorks but not to Music Planet, revealing potential outreach and digital PR opportunities.
Use both views together: Position Tracking shows how Music Planet performs against competitors; Backlink Gap helps explain an important source of competitive authority and where new links could be earned.
SEMrush does not require one generic competitor-analysis workflow. Use the competitor view inside the tools that answer the specific question. In Position Tracking, compare Music Planet with therockshop.co.nz and musicworks.co.nz across visibility, ranking distribution, keyword positions and movement over time. In Backlink Analytics and Backlink Gap, compare referring domains and link authority, then identify domains that link to competitors but not to Music Planet. The teaching distinction is simple: Position Tracking tells us who is winning in the monitored search results; backlink analysis helps explain one source of authority and highlights outreach opportunities. For Assignment 2, students should connect the evidence to a recommendation rather than merely reporting the comparison.
Step 9 of 9 · Benchmark & Monitor

Turn SEMrush into an ongoing system

In the project dashboard, configure email alerts or scheduled PDF reports for Site Audit health score and errors, and for Position Tracking changes on key keywords. Set a review cadence and use the SEO Dashboard as your at-a-glance view of visibility trends, traffic estimates, keyword movement, and open issues.

Weekly: technical health + rankings Monthly: keyword + content strategy
Why this matters: ongoing monitoring lets you show whether Music Planet's SEO health is improving or slipping, and demonstrate that awareness in your Assignment 2 audit.
Email reports to a personal address, students are not required to submit them, but receiving weekly updates keeps them passively aware of the data. Scheduled reports can be PDF or CSV, PDF is more accessible for non-technical stakeholders and is the format most likely to be used professionally. Position Tracking alerts, configure for significant changes, for example movement of plus or minus 5 positions on key terms, mirroring professional practice. SEO Dashboard widgets: Visibility Score, Traffic trend, ranking distribution, and open Site Audit issues. Assignment 2 connection: a student who can show trend data over several weeks and explain what changed and why demonstrates deeper engagement than one who ran a single one-off audit.
